Big Dipper Dialogue

Photinus pyralis — a courtship story told on six fixed LEDs
Three males flash high in the jar. The female (LED 6, lowest, "in the grass") takes a liking to the dominant male, the one with the long, slow-fading flash. A rival tries his luck and she goes quiet. The dominant male wins her back, descends, lands beside her, and the two go dark together while the rivals flash on.

      Field notes behind the story

      • Male flash. A single long yellow flash, repeated every 5–7 s on a typical evening and as fast as ~3 s on hot nights (Edmunds 1963). The story uses fixed 4, 5 and 6 s cadences so each male is easy to tell apart.
      • The dominant male has the longest flash. Females of single-flash species favor longer flashes. Here the rivals flash 0.4 s and the dominant male 0.8 s plus an extra 0.25 s on his fade.
      • Female answer. One dim flash after a species-specific delay of about 1–2 s (one measurement: 2.1 ± 0.2 s), much dimmer than a male's.
      • Rivals and approach. The male homes in on her answers and lands nearby, and rival males converge on an answering female. The rival interlude, cycle counts and LED positions in this story are design choices for the jar, not measured behavior.
