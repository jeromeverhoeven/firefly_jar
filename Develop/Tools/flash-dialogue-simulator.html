Field notes

  • Male flash: a single yellow, J-shaped flash, 0.6–0.8 s, repeated every 5–7 s, thrown during a rising hook at the end of a dipping flight — the "big dipper" flight that gives the species its common name.
  • Female: answers from a stationary perch, 1–2 s after the male's flash (commonly cited near 2.0–2.2 s at 21°C). This delay is species-specific — get it wrong and the dialogue breaks down.
  • Female flash duration is less precisely documented in the sources below; treated here as an estimate.
  • Flash rate is known to quicken in warmer air; the temperature control applies an illustrative scaling only — no precise rate law is sourced here.
