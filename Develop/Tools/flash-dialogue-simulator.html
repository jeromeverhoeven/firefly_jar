Field notes

  • In the field: the male throws a single yellow flash while flying a rising-then-dipping "J" path, repeated every 5–7 s — the "big dipper" flight that gives the species its common name.
  • In the jar: our LEDs are fixed points, not wings — there's no up-and-down flight to borrow. Instead, each male display is a fade up, hold, then fade down, standing in for the climb and dip of his flight. Each of those three segments is independently randomized 1–2 s, so no two displays read identically.
  • Female: answers from a stationary perch with a sharp 0.5 s flash, timed 2 s after the male's display begins (field-documented response delay is commonly cited near 1–2.2 s after his flash). This delay is species-specific — get it wrong and the dialogue breaks down.
  • Flash rate is known to quicken in warmer air; the temperature control applies an illustrative scaling only — no precise rate law is sourced here.
