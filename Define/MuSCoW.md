# MuSCoW

|Instructions|
|----|
|A MuSCoW mean **Mu**st, **S**hould, **Co**uld, **W**ont: each line should be a user story, constraint, criteria, feature, parameter, or something else that describes and defines you project. If it is important to the project write it down, if you don't how is the next guy supposed to know to consider it.|
|Each line must explained and then dispositioned as Must/Should/Could/Wont below are examples of each disposition.  Feel free to cut and paste the samples below|



| Num | Item | Expansion | MuSCoW | notes |
|----|----|----|----|----|
| 1 | Firefly-in-a-jar look | The product must visually read as fireflies glowing/captured inside a jar - a jar-style enclosure with warm, flickering point-light sources inside | **Must** | Core aesthetic of the product, not optional. Warm color is acceptable; exact LED color and glow pattern are left to a Design decision |
| 2 | Battery operated | The unit must run entirely on battery power, no wall adapter/mains power required | **Must** | Enables gifting/portability use case |
| 3 | Budget control: $20/unit easy yes | Total BOM cost per unit at or under $20 (qty 12) is an automatic yes. Anything above $20/unit must be justified by a clear increase in joy (item 12), a decrease in assembly time (item 18), or another stated benefit | *Should* | Cost exists to keep the budget in control, not as a hard cap - 12 cousins, unique handmade gifts, don't break the bank. Every Design decision that exceeds $20/unit must state what the extra spend buys |
| 4 | Shake to activate (accelerometer) | Device could use a low-cost accelerometer (e.g. ADXL-series) to detect motion/shaking of the jar and trigger the firefly glow effect | *Could* | More sensitive/configurable than a tilt switch, but higher part cost and needs MCU logic to debounce. Incremental cost drops to ~$0 if the chosen MCU module already includes one |
| 5 | Shake to activate (tilt/vibration switch) | Device could use a simple mechanical tilt or vibration switch (ball switch / spring switch) to detect shaking and trigger the glow effect | *Could* | Cheapest shake-detection option; binary on/off, no analog logic needed - good budget fit |
| 6 | Low-light to activate | Device could use a light sensor (e.g. photoresistor) so dim/dark ambient conditions trigger the glow effect, mimicking real fireflies at dusk | *Could* | Likely cheapest sensor option overall; good budget fit. Supports the night-light behavior in item 21 |
| 7 | Sound/clap to activate | Device could use a microphone or sound sensor so talking or clapping near the jar triggers the glow effect | *Could* | Needs a mic + simple threshold detection circuit; check cost and standby-power impact |
| 8 | PIR (motion/presence) to activate | Device could use a passive infrared (PIR) sensor to detect a person approaching/walking by and trigger the glow effect | *Could* | Distinct trigger story from shake - detects someone nearby without anyone touching the jar; check cost and whether its field-of-view/false-trigger behavior fits a small decorative object |
| 9 | Single-trigger activation | Device could use just one trigger input (one sensor from items 4-8) to activate the glow effect | *Could* | Simplest and cheapest logic; lowest part count. Note: item 21 (night light AND toy) implies at least two wake paths |
| 10 | OR'd multi-trigger activation | Device could activate the glow effect when ANY one of the selected sensors (items 4-8) fires - e.g. shake OR low-light OR sound OR presence | *Could* | More responsive/forgiving trigger behavior; needs combining logic (diode-OR or MCU) across chosen sensors |
| 11 | AND'd multi-trigger activation | Device could require that two or more of the selected sensors (items 4-8) fire together/in sequence before activating the glow effect - e.g. low-light AND shake | *Could* | More deliberate/novel trigger behavior but adds logic complexity and may need an MCU; check cost and false-trigger-avoidance tradeoff |
| 12 | Maximum, lasting joy in use | The product should bring maximum joy to the user in its use and operation - discovering/triggering the glow and watching it should feel delightful, not just functional - and that joy should last well beyond Christmas morning, not fade after the novelty wears off | *Should* | Qualitative/experiential requirement; weigh against other Design decisions (timing, LED behavior, enclosure feel). Lasting joy favors variation, surprise, hidden modes, and personalization over a single fixed effect |
| 13 | Flexible power/animation platform | The driver electronics should be flexible enough to power, light, and/or animate other small items placed in the jar - not hard-wired to only the firefly LED stems | *Should* | Pushes toward a general-purpose output (e.g. a few spare switched/PWM outputs or a simple connector header) rather than a fixed, single-purpose LED driver; impacts Design's choice of MCU I/O count and connector strategy |
| 14 | Audience: cousins ages 7-16 | The product must be appropriate, usable, and appealing for recipients from about 7 to 16 years old | **Must** | Drives safety (item 15), durability (item 17), and styling - younger kids want playful, teens must not find it babyish. Styling may vary per recipient while electronics stay common |
| 15 | Safe for the audience | The product must be safe for a 7-year-old to handle, shake, and keep in a bedroom: no accessible or swallowable batteries (no coin cells), no exposed sharp wire ends, enclosure that won't create a laceration hazard if dropped, and safe battery chemistry/charging if rechargeable | **Must** | Shake-to-activate means kids will shake the jar - glass vs. shatter-resistant enclosure must be resolved in Design, possibly per age |
| 16 | Delivered by Christmas | All units must be built, tested, and ready to give by December 25 | **Must** | Schedule constraint on part lead times, firmware scope, and build weekend; every Could is weighed against deadline risk |
| 17 | Durable | The product must survive normal kid handling for years: repeated shaking, being knocked over, and a drop from table height onto carpet, without LED stems, solder joints, or the battery coming loose | **Must** | Shake-to-activate puts continuous mechanical stress on stems and joints; verify in Destroy |
| 18 | Build time 1 hour or less | Hands-on build time per unit should be 1 hour or less, excluding one-time setup and firmware development | *Should* | Owner's time is a real cost for a 12-unit hand build; a design costing more than $20/unit can be justified by reducing this (item 3) |
| 19 | Kid can restore power | The recipient should be able to restore power themselves (recharge or replace batteries) without tools or adult help | *Should* | Drives power architecture (e.g. USB-C rechargeable vs. tool-free AA door) |
| 20 | Quantity: 12 units | One unit per cousin, 12 units total, plus spare parts for failures | **Must** | Supersedes the earlier qty-10 assumption; cost targets in item 3 are evaluated at qty 12 |
| 21 | Night light AND toy | The product must work as a night light (comes alive on its own when the room goes dark) and as a toy (the user can wake it up on demand at any time, e.g. to show people) | **Must** | Requires at least two wake paths - an ambient/dark trigger plus a user-initiated trigger. Resolve the specific sensors in DD-1 |
| 22 | User-reprogrammable | The recipient (e.g. an older cousin) could reprogram or modify the firmware/animations themselves | *Could* | Supports lasting joy (item 12) for the teens; favors an MCU programmable over USB without a separate programmer. Evaluate in the platform/MCU Design decision |
| 23 | Quiet/sleep mode for bedtime | A mode or auto-off that keeps the jar from lighting up at night when the recipient doesn't want it to | Won't | Being slightly annoying is part of the fun - not designing for this |
| 24 | Shipping/mailing | Packaging or battery choices to support mailing the gifts | Won't | Gifts are hand-delivered, not mailed - no shipping constraints on glass or lithium batteries |
